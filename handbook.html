Character Skills

Character Skills

Grit - Strength and physical power Finesse - Dexterity and fine motor skills Charm - Charisma in social situations Focus - Mental fortitude Luck - Just how lucky you are

When making an action roll, whether in combat or outside of combat, the player will “Roll (Skill)” such as “Roll Grit,” or “Roll Charm.” To do this, the player will take a number of d12s equal to their skill points in that particular skill, and roll all of those dice at once. Then the player will take the highest number they rolled as the result. There’s no math— simply roll the amount of dice equal to your skill stat and take the highest result. If the Skill you’re rolling has 0 points, Roll 2d12 and take the lower result.

For example, the player declares they want to climb a fence. To climb, you need strength, so the GM tells the player to “Roll Grit.” The player's Grit skill has 3 points, so the player will roll 3d12. The results are 6, 8, and 10. 10 is the highest result, so the player tells the GM “I rolled a 10,” and the GM will tell the player whether or not they were able to climb the fence based on if the 10 met or exceeded the secret Difficulty number the GM set prior to the roll.

Whenever a character levels up, they may add 1 point to any skill.

Receiving a Scar, item, or status effect may reduce or increase points for skills permanently or temporarily.

Rules · 2 / 22

Combat

Combat turn order is whatever makes the most narrative sense. The GM should prompt players to take a turn when they haven’t made any moves in a while, and have the enemy NPCs jump in when it makes sense.

● On a player's turn, the player has 2 Action Points they can spend. Attacking and moving are the two most common actions, though the GM can determine if what a player wants to do will take an Action Point. ○ Players may not attack twice in the same turn.

○ Using a magic item usually requires an Action Point. ○ Speaking does not cost an Action Point, unless the character is attempting to bargain with or intimidate another character. If a character’s speech triggers a roll of some kind, it costs an action point.

Rules · 3 / 22

Evasion

Every character has an Evasion score determined by their Finesse + Luck+ 2. Evasion is a character’s ability to avoid incoming attacks and is represented by a number that the attacker must beat on their attack roll.

Some items may temporarily raise a character’s Evasion against certain kinds of attacks. If an item, potion, etc., raises a character’s Finesse or Luck, the character’s Evasion remains unchanged.

Wearing armor may raise Evasion. Even though armor would make a character slower depending on how heavy it is, it adds protection and is in and of itself a way to “evade” damage. If a character is wearing armor and is attacked, if the attacker’s roll doesn’t match or beat the armor-wearer’s Evasion score, the GM may flavor this attack as having simply bounced off the armor as opposed to having missed entirely. For this reason, wearing armor can raise Evasion.

Parry: If the attacking player’s highest roll matches the defending character's Evasion, the defending player may make an attack against the attacking player. This does not count as the defending players turn and does not cost an Action Point.

Attacking

When a character makes an attack, they roll the Skill that makes the most sense for that kind of attack. - If the result of the attack roll beats the defending character’s Evasion, the defending player takes damage equal to the weapon’s Damage Rating. Every weapon has a Damage Rating. If you are using an improvised weapon such as a rock or plank of wood, consult with the GM to determine which attack stat you would roll and its damage number. - If the attacking character’s roll is less than the defending character’s Evasion, the attacking character “missed.” - If the attack roll matches the defending character’s Evasion, the attack deals no damage and the defending character gets an opportunity to Parry.

For example: Player A attacks Enemy B with a hammer. Because it’s a hammer, the GM would direct Player A to Roll Grit. Player A has 2 Grit, so they roll 2d12 and get a 4 and an 8. Because 8 is higher, we take that as the result. Then the GM compares Player A’s 8 against Enemy B’s

Evasion. Enemy B’s Evasion is 5, and 8 is greater than 5, so the attack hits. The hammer has a Damage Number of 4, so Enemy B takes 4 damage.

Rules · 4 / 22

Health and Rests

● A character’s maximum number of Health Points is determined by their Grit + Character Level + 10. ○ If a player is able to boost their particular Skill score using temporary means such as potions, spells, or items, do not re-calculate Maximum Health. The only time Health should be recalculated is when a character's Skill score is changed due to leveling up or scars. ● Each rest, characters roll Focus and gain Health Points back equal to the highest result. ● Character’s cannot have a number of Health Points that exceeds their Maximum Health Score.

Character Classes · 5 / 22

Classes

Below are the classes you can choose to play as. Though some may imply a job, you do not have to play it that way. Work with the GM to determine how you might play a certain class without fitting into the stereotypical mold of that class. For example: you may choose the knight class, but you don’t have to wear armor, ride horses, and stick to a code of chivalry. You can use the knight class but look like a caveman, if you wanted— enjoying the perks of the knight class without ever calling yourself a “knight” within the narrative.

As you level up, you will unlock more abilities. When you unlock more abilities this way, you still get to use the old abilities. It’s important to note that there are some abilities that are upgraded as your character levels up. These do not stack— the higher level ability simply replaces the lower level ability of the same name.

Magister

Character Creation:

- Choose 3 potions from the Potions Table. You begin the game with these potions. - Choose 3 potion recipes. These do not have to be the same as the 3 potions you start the game with. You begin the game with these recipes in your inventory and can brew these potions during Rests if you have the ingredients. See the Brewmaster ability for more information. As you go on your journey, you may look for more potion recipes to add to your collection. You also begin the game with the ingredients for these 3 recipes.

Character Level 1:

- Brewmaster: Once per rest, you can choose to either 1) brew 1 potion of your choice that you have the recipe and ingredients for, or 2) look for potion ingredients to refill your stock. If you brew a potion, you no longer have the ingredients to brew that specific potion again until you find more ingredients. If you want to look for ingredients, name the potion you want to find ingredients for and Roll Luck to determine if you find them. On a success, you find the ingredients. If you choose to brew a potion or look for ingredients, you do not gain Health from the Rest. - Magister’s Guild: +1 Charm when speaking with other Magisters. The GM may revoke this if the other Magister is hostile toward you.

Character Level 3:

- Potion-Throwing Experience: You get +1 Focus when throwing potions.

Character Level 5:

- Brewmaster 2: Once per rest, you can choose to either 1) brew 2 potions of your choice that you have the recipe and ingredients for, or 2) look for potion ingredients to refill your stock. If you brew a potion, you no longer have the ingredients to brew that specific potion again until you find more ingredients. If you want to look for ingredients, name the two potions you want to find ingredients for and Roll Luck to determine if you find them. On a success, you find the ingredients. If you choose to brew potions or look for ingredients, you do not gain Health from the Rest.

Character Level 6:

- Master Potion Designer: You’ve developed a way to craft potion bottles that block the potion in a certain direction. When you activate a potion by throwing it, you may declare one target in the potions range of effect that is not affected.

Character Level 8:

- Brewmaster 3: Once per rest, you can choose to either 1) brew 3 potions of your choice that you have the recipe and ingredients for, or 2) look for potion ingredients to refill your stock. If you brew a potion, you no longer have the ingredients to brew that specific potion again until you find more ingredients. If you want to look for ingredients, name the three potions you want to find ingredients for and Roll Luck to determine if you find them. On a success, you find the ingredients. If you choose to brew 3 potions or look for 3 sets of ingredients, you do not gain Health from the Rest, but if you only brew 1 potion or look for ingredients for 1 potion, you may still gain Health from the Rest.

- The character cannot move on their own. - The character's Evasion is set to their Luck score.

- If the character’s health reaches 0, they die instantly.

Drunk

- The character has +1 Charm when dealing with friendly people, and -1 Charm when dealing with those who wouldn’t take kindly to a drunkard. Consult the GM to determine whether or not you receive +1 or -1 Charm, depending on the NPC you’re speaking to.

Exhausted

- -1 Grit, -1 Finesse, -1 Focus - Usually cured by a rest.

Distracted

- -2 Focus

Freezing

- -2 Finesse, -1 Focus - Cured by finding warmth by a fire or in a building.

Starving

- -1 Grit, -1 Focus. - Lose 1 Health periodically. Consult the GM to determine how often you lose Health.

Pinned

- You cannot move. You may roll Grit to see if you break free. Consult the GM to determine when you can roll Grit this way.

Rules · 14 / 22

Death

When a character’s health is reduced to zero or below, they are knocked unconscious. If in combat, on their next turn, they roll Luck. - If the result is 9-12, the character awakens with 5 health, the “Beaten” and “Exhausted” statuses, and takes a scar. - If the result is 5-8, the character awakens with 1 Health, the “Beaten,” and “Paralyzed” statuses, and takes a scar. - If the result is 1-4, the character dies.

Potions

Every Potion has a recipe and ingredients that are needed before the potion can be brewed (created). The recipe and ingredients are designed to be vague/amorphous so there’s no need to come up with or know specifics like “3 Oak Leaves, one frog eyeball, etc.,” or “boil for 6 minutes.” You simply need to know you have the recipe and the ingredients. Magisters are the only people with the brewing know-how, so if you aren’t a magister, you’ll need to find one to have potions created. Or you can simply find potions in the world or buy them in shops. You can sell recipes and ingredients if you can’t find a magister to utilize them.

Healing Potion

- Can be activated by being drunk or poured on a wound. - Roll Focus and take the lowest result. Gain that much health. Frost Potion - Wherever the liquid touches, shards of ice appear and expand out, like an explosion of ice. - Throw this at a target. If you beat the target’s Evasion by 1-3, the target receives the “Freezing” status. If you beat it by 4 or more, they are also trapped by ice shards and receive the “Pinned” status. Light Potion - Shake the bottle to activate. - Light illuminates from the bottle like a torch, but without the danger of a flame. Fire Potion - Usually thrown at enemies, wherever this potion shatters, a ball of flame erupts. - Damage: 5 Softening Potion - Whatever this liquid touches is softened. Try it on locks to easily break them, or on a dragon’s scales to more easily pierce through. Consult the GM to determine its effectiveness. Cobweb Potion - Whatever this liquid touches is trapped in a sticky cobweb, and receives the “Pinned” Status. It can be poured out or thrown. Flare Potion - Set this off by smashing it on the ground. Rage Potion - Drink to activate. - You receive +2 Grit, +1 Evasion, +1 Finesse when attacking, +3 Health, and +3 Maximum Health for 20 minutes. - After this potion's effects wear off, lose 8 Health Points. Rules · 17 / 22

Scars

When a character wakes up after having their health reduced to zero, the player will roll 1d12 and receive a Scar from the table below. Scars can be physical or mental.

1. You’re haunted by Death and it doesn’t leave your mind. -1 Charm. 2. Now knowing the fragility of life, you value it in yourself and others. +1 Charm. 3. Your injury causes a limp or bum shoulder (or whatever body part was affected). -1 Grit. 4. What doesn’t kill you makes you stronger. +1 Grit. 5. It hurts to lift your arms above your head. -1 Finesse. 6. Life’s too short. Let’s get moving. +1 Finesse 7. That was a sign your Luck has begun to run out. -1 Luck. 8. You should’ve died. You’re pretty lucky. +1 Luck. 9. The pain is distracting. -1 Focus. 10. You won’t let that happen again. +1 Focus 11. You might never fully recover. -3 Maximum Health. 12. You feel better than ever. +3 Maximum Health

Remember to recalculate Health and/or Evasion when Permanent Stat points change. You should record what Scar you received in case you forget why you have 1 less Finesse, or 3 extra Health points.

Game Master · 20 / 22

GM NOTES

- This game is designed to be rules-light and give you and the players freedom to craft a narrative together as you play. However, some classes, namely Huntsman, Merchant, and Noble, have abilities that rely on you, the GM, to remember during play. - If you have players who choose Huntsman, you’ll need to remember to introduce “hunger” and the “starving” status. Many GMs opt to ignore hunger and eating in game, but one ability, “Hunting Trip,” would be useless if hunger was ignored. You can use hunger to prompt side missions to get food which can be naturally spun off into full on campaigns. When used correctly, it’s a simple yet effective motivator that feels realistic and gets the players moving toward a simple goal that you can use to introduce them to new NPCs, areas of intrigue, and danger. Additionally, if you have a Huntsman in the party, you’ll need to introduce wildlife fairly often so they can utilize their other abilities. - If you have a player that chooses Merchant, you’ll need to pay more attention to in-game currency. This game is designed to keep money simple, so it’s suggested to simply use 1 currency “coin” and have items cost the same as they would in the real world. This streamlines currency and negates the need for conversion tables that bring the game to a stop. Remember to give the merchant opportunities to sell their wares, and put the party in situations where they need to pay money, or maybe even have their money stolen from them. - If you have a player who chooses Noble, you will need to consider how every NPC would react around them. A Noble is essentially a celebrity. At character creation, discuss with the player how well-known they might be. Are they a Lord over a small town? Or are they a prince over the entire nation? These questions

would determine how often they are recognized. Allow them to be recognized fairly often so they can use their Charm buff. And in combat, allow enemies to be scared to hurt them, as notated in their “Nobility Armor” ability.

Game Master · 21 / 22

Leveling Up

Allowing characters to level up should be rare. Characters shouldn’t level up every session. This is because there is a limit to how high stats can go before a d12 has no effect on them. Let leveling up be a big moment to celebrate. Maybe even allow characters to level up mid-combat for an exciting moment. Don’t be afraid to let 1 character level up and not others if they pull off something truly amazing— if your players are ok with being out of sync with each other in terms of their level.

Game Master · 22 / 22

Potions and Magic

There are no magic spells in this world. Magic is housed in potions and magical items only. This is why the magic class is called Magister and not Wizard or Sorcerer. Study the potion table and feel free to create your own. Let potions be common loot, especially if you have a Magister in the party. There’s no hard limit on how many potions a character can carry, but if you want to limit it, feel free to give the characters/players a max number of potions they can carry at one time.

If a Magister player is confused by the Brewmaster ability, explain that their ingredients bag is essentially a checklist of all the potion ingredients. “Ingredients” is a purposefully vague/amorphous term and you do not have to name specific ingredients. So if the Magister wants to use the Brewmaster ability to brew a Cobweb Potion, for example, ask them if they have the Cobweb ingredients. If they do, then they mark off those ingredients (and they no longer have them) and they now have the potion. If they don’t have the ingredients, then they’ll have to go collect the ingredients instead. Ask the player if they want to look for Cobweb Potion ingredients or brew a different potion. When they look for ingredients, they should name the potion they are looking for ingredients for. On a successful roll, they find the ingredients for that potion only. If they search for ingredients during a Rest, they cannot also brew potions during that same Rest.

Attacks

- You’ll have to improvise the Damage Number of improvised weapons, such as rocks or planks of wood. You can use the weapons table in this document to find an established weapon and use its stats for an improvised weapon that is similar. If the player is throwing something, they should roll focus, even if they are throwing a sword (that uses

Grit to attack). Focus in this scenario represents the character’s aim when throwing something. - If you wish to introduce shields or Armor to the game, have them simply reduce Damage taken. Do not raise Evasion. - If an attack would not realistically make sense, it’s the GM’s responsibility to inform the player that it would not deal damage. For example, if a player wants to punch a fully armored knight with their bare hand, it would not make sense for the player to deal damage to the armored target. In game, this would look like a player saying “I punch the knight,” and instead of the GM saying “Ok, roll Grit,” the GM would describe how the player punches it and doesn’t leave a dent. The punch may even harm the attacker. The narrative should override the rules as written when necessary. Alternatively, you could have a successful attack not deal damage to the knight, but maybe there is still a positive outcome, such as the knight being knocked down, or his helmet flying off. - With Ranged weapons that fire ammunition, such as a bow firing arrows, you do not have to have your players keep track of the ammo. Because this game is rules-light, feel free to allow your players to have infinite ammo. However, if they throw a dagger or their mace, they should have to pick it back up after the fight. Be lenient with your players and if they forget to pick it up, just ret-con the story and say “yeah you grabbed it as you left,” unless they were fleeing from a burning building or an avalanche. - If the world you create includes other fantasy races and your players want to play as them, work with them in session zero to come up with a few abilities their race grants them. Be very careful not to increase Evasion unless it’s only raised for a specific situation like “while you’re in water, Evasion +1” or “When being attacked with a long range weapon, +1 Evasion.” Think outside the box for abilities such as “can speak to plants,” or “can breathe under water,” or “once per rest, you can command someone to take an action. The target rolls Focus and if they fail, they do what you say to the best of their ability.”
